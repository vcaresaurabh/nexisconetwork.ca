Changes made to nexisconetwork.ca before the Google Ads appeal (account suspended under the **Unacceptable Business
Practices** policy). The site now represents Nexisco Network Inc. accurately and consistently: the correct legal identity
everywhere, nothing that implies clients, results, staff or capacity the business can't show, and the same content for
every visitor, including Google's crawlers.

- Branch: `pre-appeal-fixes` on top of `main` at `94dd99e`. **Not deployed.** Commits: Phase 1 `fa1d5fb` · Phase 2
  `1576f20` · Phase 3 `a5ebb8d` + `b28fd5f` (consistency fixes) · Phase 4 `7ccaa2b` · Phase 5 `a2ecb41` · final-review

## Before you turn ads back on

**Set up email and the database so the forms can deliver.** The forms used to throw every submission away while
telling visitors "we'll reply within 1 hour". They now work properly (see "Follow-up fixes" below), but they need two
things in the server's `public_html/config/config.php`:

1. **SMTP** — `smtp.host`, `smtp.port`, `smtp.user`, `smtp.pass`, `smtp.from_address` for a real mailbox on the
   domain. For GoDaddy that is `smtpout.secureserver.net`, port `465` (SSL is used automatically on 465; set
   `secure` to `tls` for port 587).
2. **Database** — create a MySQL database, import `sql/schema.sql`, and set `db.user` / `db.pass` (and `db.name`). The
   newsletter needs it for the CASL consent record; contact and partner forms also keep a copy there, and the admin
   panel lists them.

Until both are set, each form shows an honest error with the support email and phone number instead of a fake "thanks",
and the footer shows "write to support@… to subscribe" instead of a signup form. After setting them, send one test
contact message, one quote request, one partner application and one newsletter sign-up (click the confirmation link)
before running ads. Also set `app.admin_pass` to a `password_hash()` value if you want the admin panel.

When you send newsletters (from any tool), email only rows with `subscribed = 1 AND casl_consent = 1`, and put each
person's own link `https://nexisconetwork.ca/newsletter/unsubscribe?token=<unsub_token>` in every email — the Opt-In
policy promises that link. Delete sign-ups that were never confirmed (`subscribed = 0 AND casl_consent = 0`) after about
30 days.

**The live server doesn't match this repo — check the hosting before you appeal.** A read-only request to
https://nexisconetwork.ca on 20 October 2026 showed:

- a `Content-Security-Policy` header that is **not** the one in this repo's `.htaccess`, and that allow-lists live-chat
  vendors (`code.jivosite.com`, `*.jivosite.com`, `*.jivo.chat`, `chatresque.com`). Nothing in this repo uses them. Find
  where that header comes from (another `.htaccess` above `public_html`, cPanel, or an older deployed copy), remove it,
  and make sure no chat widget is running on the domain;
- a GoDaddy traffic script (`img1.wsimg.com/traffic-assets/js/tccl.min.js`) injected after `</body>` by the hosting
  platform. It isn't in the repo; turn it off in the hosting settings if you don't use it.

Until this branch is deployed, the live site still shows everything this document says was removed.

## Decisions made — please confirm before deploying

These are the judgment calls made while working without stopping between phases. Each one can be changed in one place.

1. **PRIVACY_CONTACT was still a placeholder** ("name + email for privacy requests"). The Privacy Policy now names
   **Aman Deshwal, Director, at support@nexisconetwork.ca** as the person accountable under PIPEDA. The unconfirmed
   `privacy@nexisconetwork.ca` address was removed. To change it, edit `privacy_contact` in `config/business.php`.
2. **SITE_OWNERSHIP is rendered verbatim**: "owned by nexisconetwork.ca, with all content and code licensed to the client
   on completion". It applies to websites delivered under a monthly plan. One-time project work keeps the existing rule:
   the client owns the deliverables on full payment. Terms §5, the FAQ and the service pages are now scoped this way.
   Note that nexisconetwork.ca is a domain, not a legal person. You may want "owned by Nexisco Network Inc." instead
   (one line in `business.php`). Before this change, the site told every client "you own it" with "no lock-in".
3. **CANCELLATION "14 days" means 14 days' written notice** to cancel a monthly plan or retainer. The Cancellation Policy
   previously said 30 days. After the 1-month minimum term, plans continue month to month.
4. **HOURS** "Mon–SAT" is displayed as "Mon–Sat".
5. **Wording swaps beyond the explicit list**, made because they imply clients or capability the business can't show:
   - "Most Popular" (plan badge and Partners model) → "Recommended"
   - "Expert web development" → "Professional web development"
   - "Why clients choose Nexisco" → "Why work with Nexisco"
   - "The growth team your brand actually needs" → "The growth partner…"
   - outcome promises in the service copy ("rank well", "measurable ROI", "fastest path to growth") → descriptions of
     the work
6. **Stock photos**: the group-of-people photo is replaced with a people-free laptop/analytics photo everywhere: the
   former "team" image plus the decorative About, Start a Project, Partners and footer backgrounds. The homepage hero
   (a monitor showing a website design that the old portfolio presented as a client project, with an HP logo) is now an
   abstract white-waves image. The remaining decorative backgrounds (hands at a whiteboard, a blurred meeting table,
   a card reader) keep `alt=""` and are not labelled or captioned. The Ecommerce service image was also one of the old
   portfolio "projects"; it is now only a decorative service background, but you may want to swap it too.
   The social-share image (`og-image.png`) said "US · Canada · Worldwide | Billed in USD & CAD"; it was re-rendered in
   the same design with the Edmonton / Canada & the US positioning, "Billed in USD or CAD" and the legal name.
7. **Kept as written**:
   - "We reply within 1 business day" (Partners applications)
   - "quote … usually within 1–2 business days" (Start a Project)
   - Terms §5 "Portfolio rights", which still mentions "portfolio, case studies" for future work, as instructed
   - the payment methods listed in the footer (cards, PayPal, bank/wire transfer), which aren't in the FACTS block

### Legal points only you (or counsel) can decide

- **Payment methods** (cards, PayPal, bank/wire, "PCI-DSS compliant processors") are not in the FACTS block, and the
  footer's own note says the processors aren't confirmed yet. They were left as written in the visible copy and removed
  from the structured data. Confirm them.
- **CAD prices** in `data/pricing.php` are the same numbers as the USD prices. The homepage now says "CAD pricing on the
  Pricing page" — confirm the CAD amounts.
- **Terms §13** describes SMS STOP/HELP keywords for an SMS program the Opt-In page says doesn't exist. **Cancellation
  §1** lets a signed SOW override the policy, including the 14-day notice. **Enterprise "Priority Support"** reads like
  a support-desk promise; consider "Priority scheduling".
- **Timelines** ("most marketing websites in 3–6 weeks") are estimates, not a record — fine as long as they're realistic.

- **Liability caps differ.** Disclaimer §8 caps liability at "the amount actually paid … for the specific service".
  Terms §10 caps it at fees paid "during the three (3) months" before the claim.
- **Showing client work.** Terms §5 "Portfolio rights" (kept as instructed) lets us showcase delivered work unless the
  client opts out in writing. The new compliance rules only allow client-approved work, so you may want §5 to require
  written approval instead.
- **Reporting promises vs plan features.** The homepage, FAQ and Digital Marketing page promise plain-English monthly
  reports (and a live analytics dashboard for marketing clients), but on the pricing page "Monthly Reports" appears only
  in Advanced and "Custom Analytics Dashboard" only in Enterprise. Confirm every client gets monthly reports, or narrow
  the wording.
- "Hashed credentials" was removed from Privacy §10 because nothing is hashed (the admin password is compared in plain
  text — see below).
